Privacy Policy for GroupIt

Zero Plus Plus Studios · Last updated 2 October 2026 · zeroplusplusstudios@gmail.com

The short version

GroupIt still keeps your progress on your own phone, and we still have no account of our own, no sign-in and no server of our own holding anything about you. Your phone's own iCloud or Google backup can carry that progress to a new phone, and on an iPhone between your own devices, under your own account with Apple or Google; we have no access to it.

What is new in this version is that the game can show you an advert you chose to watch, and can sell a few optional extras. Both of those involve other companies, and this policy exists to say precisely which ones, precisely what each one receives, and precisely how to stop it.

What changed, and what did not

Earlier versions of GroupIt could not reach the internet at all: the permission was stripped out of the app before it was built. That is no longer true, because an advert has to be fetched and a purchase has to be checked with the shop that sold it.

What did not change is the game. Every board is still solvable without paying and without watching anything. Hints are still there: free and unlimited on the ladder that teaches the game, and elsewhere the first hint on every board is free and later ones cost gems, which new boards you finish pay. The extras are gems, shortcuts and colours, and no puzzle, board or star sits behind any of them.

What the app stores, and where

The game saves your progress on your own device: where you stand on the ladder, the boards you have solved in free play and on the daily, which rule pages you have seen, the awards you have collected, and your settings for sound, music, vibration, marking mode, counters and day mode. If you played a version from before October 2026, your save may also still hold that version's chapter progress — the stars each chapter board earned, the last one you played and the drawings you named. The game no longer adds to those, but a new install is not always free of them: they can arrive with the rest of your save — restored from your device's backup, or on an iPhone copied across from your other devices through iCloud. Erase all progress clears them.

This version adds a few more local records. One is a list of which extras this device owns, written one entry per product when a purchase completes and when the shop restores an earlier one, beside a short list of the shop's order numbers for gem packs, kept so that a purchase the shop delivers twice is only counted once. Your gems are kept here too, with a note of which boards you have already had your free hint on. So is the last settings file the game fetched (see "What the app sends") and when; how many adverts you have watched for gems today, so the daily limit holds; and which days' daily boards have already paid out, so that a changed phone clock cannot pay one twice.

All of it is kept in the app's private storage on your phone. It is not an account, it is not linked to you, and no one else — including us — can read it. Uninstalling the app deletes it.

Your progress also follows you to a new phone, and on an iPhone between your own devices, and the way it does that differs by platform. On an iPhone, the game uses Apple's iCloud key-value storage: it writes your progress — the ladder, free play, the daily and the endless shelf, your awards, your gems, and the modes and colours you have bought with them — to your own iCloud, and reads back what your other iPhones and iPads on the same iCloud account wrote, so a second device picks up where the first left off. Your settings stay on each device. On an Android phone, the game uses Android's Auto Backup, which copies the game's one save file, holding your progress and your settings, to your Google Drive so that a new phone or a reinstall gets it back; it does not sync between devices as you play.

In both cases it happens under your own account with Apple or Google and under their terms; we have no access to it, and no server of our own is involved. It carries your progress, not your purchases: those come back from the App Store or Google Play when you tap Restore purchases. You can switch it off in your device's own settings, under iCloud on an iPhone or under Backup on Android. On an iPhone, erasing your progress (see below) erases it on your other devices on the same iCloud account too, which is deliberate.

The game also keeps a plain list of what happened while you played — a board was started, a hint was asked for, a board was finished with two stars, an extra was bought — and a random identifier generated the first time the app runs, which is not derived from you or from your phone and is not an advertising id. Both live in the app's private storage. That list still goes nowhere. There is no analytics service in this app, no crash reporter, and nothing that uploads it; it exists so that a later version can be improved with some idea of where people get stuck, and the only way to read it is with the phone plugged into a computer. It is capped at five hundred entries and the oldest are dropped. Uninstalling removes all of it.

What the app sends, and who receives it

Three things leave the phone by the game's own doing in this version, and nothing else does — apart from the copy your own iCloud or Google account makes of your save, described above, which your phone does and we do not see.

A settings request, each time the app starts and again if you come back to it after an hour or more away. The app asks our website, hosted on GitHub Pages, for one small file that is the same for every player and says whether the shop and the adverts are switched on. The request carries nothing about you — no identifier, no account, no progress. As with any web request, GitHub, which runs that website, receives your device's IP address and the basic technical information every web request carries, under GitHub's own privacy statement; we do not receive it and do not keep it. The answer is saved on your phone. The time the website reports in its reply is also used, on the phone, to know what day it is, so that the daily advert limit cannot be reset by changing the phone's clock.

GitHub's privacy statement:

https://docs.github.com/site-policy/privacy-policies/github-general-privacy-statement

An advert request, when the advert system is running. This goes to Unity, and through Unity to whichever advertising company wins the auction to fill it. What it carries is described in the next section.

A purchase, when you buy one of the extras. This goes to Google Play on an Android phone or to the App Store on an iPhone — the shop you installed the game from — and it is that shop, not us, that takes the payment.

Nothing else is sent. We send no analytics, no crash reports, no telemetry of our own and nothing about how you play.

Adverts, and what Unity receives

Adverts in GroupIt are rewarded adverts only. There is no advert between boards, no banner and no full-screen interruption: the only advert in the game is one you asked for, by tapping a control that says what you get for watching. It is offered when you do not have enough gems for something, pays a few gems when it has been watched to the end, and can be watched only a few times a day. If you never tap it, you are never shown one, and one that does not load or that you close early costs you nothing and pays nothing.

They are served by Unity LevelPlay, the advertising platform operated by Unity Technologies, which acquired ironSource — you may see either name. LevelPlay is a mediation platform, which means it runs an auction between several advertising networks and the winner supplies the advert you actually see. That winning network receives the same information LevelPlay does, under its own privacy policy, and it is not always the same company twice.

What is sent with an advert request comes from your device rather than from the game: your advertising identifier (see below), your IP address, and technical facts about the phone — its make and model, the operating system version, the language and country it is set to, the screen size, the network type, and the name and version of this app. Your IP address is how an approximate location is worked out, usually to a city or a region, which is what decides whether you see an advert for something that exists near you. What happens to the advert afterwards is also reported: that it was requested, that it was shown, that it was watched to the end or abandoned, and that it was tapped.

We send the advert system nothing about how you play. Not which board you are on, not your stars, not your settings, not your progress, not the random identifier described above. The game asks it for an advert and is told whether the reward was earned, and that is the whole of the conversation.

The advert system is started when the game first needs one, and from that point it may contact its servers before you have chosen to watch anything, because an advert has to be fetched in advance to be ready to play.

Purchases, and what Apple and Google receive

The extras are sold by the shop, not by us. On an Android phone that is Google Play; on an iPhone it is the App Store. The payment happens in their own sheet, on their own systems, and the game never sees and never receives your card number, your name, your billing address or your email address. There is no field in this app that could accept them.

The extras are of three kinds. Gem packs are consumable: each purchase adds its gems to the wallet on your phone and can be bought again. Unlocks — every mode, unlimited hints, or everything together — are bought once and kept. A tip is a purchase that unlocks nothing at all; it exists only to say thank you. What the game receives back is the shop's confirmation that an order exists, and what it does with that is add the pack's gems, or write a flag in its own private storage saying this device owns the unlock. What we receive, as the developer, is the shop's reporting on sales: order numbers, dates, prices, the country a sale was made in, and refund and chargeback information where there is any. Apple and Google decide what those reports contain. Neither of them gives us your name, your address or your payment details, and we have never asked for them.

Restore purchases — the control in Settings, and in the shop screen — asks the shop what your account already owns and rewrites the local flags from the answer. It restores unlocks, not gems: a gem pack is used up the moment its gems are added, which is how the shops define a consumable purchase. That question goes to Apple or Google, who already know, and not to us.

The advertising identifier

Every modern phone carries an advertising identifier: a random number the operating system gives to apps, which advertising companies use to count how often an advert has been shown and to decide which one to show next. It is the one identifier this app allows off the device, and it is supplied by the phone rather than by the game.

On Android it is the Google Advertising ID. You can reset it, or delete it entirely, in Settings → Google → Ads. Deleting it means apps are given nothing in its place, and the adverts you see stop being chosen using it.

On an iPhone it is the IDFA, and it is only available to an app that has asked you and been told yes. GroupIt asks with the standard system prompt the first time it would matter. If you say no, you still get the reward and you still get an advert — it is simply chosen without knowing anything about you. You can change your answer at any time in Settings → Privacy & Security → Tracking.

Your choice, and the law we rely on

If you are in the United Kingdom, the European Economic Area or Switzerland, you are asked before the first advert request whether adverts may be personalised, using a consent form supplied by the advertising platform. Nothing is sent for advertising purposes until you have answered, and you can change the answer whenever you like from the privacy control in the game's Settings, which reopens the same form. Refusing costs you nothing in the game: the rewards are identical and the adverts are simply not personalised.

We rely on your consent for advertising and for the advertising identifier, and only on your consent. We rely on the performance of a contract with you for handling a purchase and for restoring one you already made — you asked to buy something, and delivering it is the contract. The record the shops keep of a sale, they keep under their own legal obligations as the seller.

We do not profile you, we do not make automated decisions about you, and we do not combine anything across apps. We could not: nothing about how you play this game leaves the phone it is played on.

If you are in California, or another US state with a privacy law

Showing you a personalised advert means an identifier about your device is passed to advertising companies. Under California law that counts as sharing for cross-context behavioural advertising, and several other states use similar language for the same thing.

We do not sell personal information for money, and never have. To opt out of the sharing: on an iPhone, decline the tracking prompt described above, or turn it off in Settings; on Android, delete or reset the advertising ID in Settings → Google → Ads. Both are honoured by the advertising platform. You can also simply never tap a control that offers an advert. If you would rather send the request in writing, email us at zeroplusplusstudios@gmail.com and we will pass it on.

How long anything is kept

On your phone, until you erase it or uninstall the app. The event list is capped at five hundred entries and drops the oldest.

By Unity and the advertising networks, for as long as their own published retention schedules say. We hold no copy of anything an advert request sent, and no way to query it — we are not given it in the first place. Unity's policy, linked above, is the document that states the periods.

By Apple and Google, the record of a sale, for as long as their terms and the tax law that applies to a seller require. We see that only as a report, and only in the form they choose to show it.

By us, nothing. There is no database, no server and no account system anywhere in this studio that holds a record of a GroupIt player.

Your rights, and how to erase what exists

If you are in the UK or the EEA you have the right to ask for access to your personal data, to have it corrected or erased, to object to or restrict how it is processed, to receive it in a portable form, and to withdraw consent at any time. Withdrawing consent does not undo what was lawfully done before you withdrew it. Similar rights apply under a number of US state laws.

Write to zeroplusplusstudios@gmail.com and we will reply within one month. Please say which right you are exercising and which phone and country you are writing about; because there is no account here, that is genuinely all we have to go on, and we will tell you honestly if the answer is that we hold nothing.

Three of these you can exercise faster yourself, without asking anybody:

Settings → Erase all progress clears your progress — the ladder, free play, the daily, the pages seen, any chapter progress kept from an older version — and the awards you have earned. It does not clear the event list, the install identifier, your gems, or the extras you have bought — an erase is meant to give you a fresh start, not to take away something you paid for — nor the record of free hints used, adverts watched today and daily boards paid, which exist so that erasing cannot be used to repeat a reward.

Resetting or deleting your advertising identifier, in the device settings named above, severs the link between you and anything an advert request has already sent. That is the single most effective thing you can do, and it takes effect immediately and everywhere, not just here.

Uninstalling the app removes everything it put on your phone, including the event list, the identifier and the record of what you own. Your purchases are held by your Apple or Google account rather than by the app, so reinstalling and tapping Restore purchases brings them back.

For anything already held by Unity or by an advertising network, the request has to go to them, because we cannot identify you to them and cannot make it on your behalf. Unity publishes a data-request route in its privacy policy at the address above; what their form asks for is the app it concerns, which is com.zeroplusplus.groupit, and we will supply anything else it needs if you ask us.

If you think we have handled this badly, you can complain to a supervisory authority. In the UK that is the Information Commissioner's Office:

https://ico.org.uk

In the EEA it is the authority for the country you live in.

Children

GroupIt is a general-audience logic puzzle and is not directed at children under 13. The advertising platform is set up to treat this app as not directed to children, which is the truthful answer for it, and the app is not enrolled in any families advertising programme.

We do not knowingly collect anything about a child under 13. If you believe a child has used this app and an advertising identifier was collected, delete or reset that identifier in the device settings — which stops it at once — and email us at zeroplusplusstudios@gmail.com so that we can ask the advertising platform to delete what it holds.

Permissions

On an Android phone this version requests four things, and each has a reason it cannot be dropped.

Vibrate, for the short tick when you mark a cell. You can turn vibration off in Settings, and the permission carries no information about you.

Internet and network state, which earlier versions of GroupIt did not have and actively removed. The settings file has to be fetched, an advert has to be fetched and a purchase has to be verified with the shop that sold it; none of those is possible offline. The game itself still needs no connection to play — every board is solved on the device, and the app works fully in aeroplane mode apart from the shop and the adverts.

The advertising ID permission on Android, which is what lets the advertising platform read the identifier described above. On an iPhone the equivalent is not a permission but the tracking prompt, which asks you directly.

Billing, on Android, which is Google Play's own permission for buying something inside an app.

On an iPhone there is no permission list to show: the tracking prompt described above is the only question the game asks the system, and the iCloud storage described under "What the app stores" needs no prompt because it is your own account's own storage.

Changes

If what this app sends ever changes again, this policy will be updated before that version is published, and the change will be described in the release notes on the store you installed it from. The date at the top moves with the text — an unchanged date on changed text is the one thing in here that could be read as bad faith.
